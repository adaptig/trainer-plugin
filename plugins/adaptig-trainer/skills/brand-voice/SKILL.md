# Brand voice for trainers

Before describing Adaptig or its workshop methods, read [the trainer context snapshot](references/trainer-context.md). Use current approved workshop materials for exact product details; the snapshot supplies orientation, not commercial authority.

For colours, typography, logos, icons, shapes and trainer co-branding, use the bundled
`branding-guidelines` skill. It supplies the visual rules and actual files in this same
base plugin; no separate Brand or Context installation is required.

Write clearly enough that the reader knows what this means for their work and what to
do next. Adaptig's existing voice is direct, specific and slightly dry. Match the trainer's
personal phrasing where it is supplied; do not make every trainer sound identical.

## Start from the job

Use the context already provided: audience, purpose, channel, language, facts and desired
action. Ask one concise question only when an omission prevents a useful draft. If a
minor detail is unknown, use an obvious placeholder and identify it in the handover.

Establish who is speaking. A certified trainer may describe their own workshop and
experience. Do not speak as company leadership, issue policy, or imply authority to
agree company pricing, contracts, certification or product promises.

## Voice

- Use familiar words, short paragraphs and concrete examples. Put the useful point first.
- Prefer a verb to an abstract noun. Describe what participants do and leave with.
- Second person works for instructions; first person works for a trainer's own post.
- Dry humour is welcome when it suits the writer; never at a participant's expense.
- Avoid decorative claims such as "seamless", "next-gen", "unlock", "supercharge" or
  "10x". Replace them with an observable action or remove them.
- Remove stock openings, inflated praise, empty conclusions and repetitive lists.
- Preserve the writer's natural wording. Tighten a draft without inventing a personal story.

## By channel

**Client email:** a clear subject, two or three short paragraphs and one main ask. When
a date is confirmed, use the absolute date. For a follow-up, separate what was agreed
from a suggestion and make ownership clear. Sign with the sender's supplied identity.
If HTML is requested, use one accent action and the real logo once; visual guidance
ships with the companion `branding-guidelines` skill.

**Workshop description or handout:** state who it is for, the work participants will do,
and the artifact or capability they should leave with. Keep proposed learning outcomes
distinct from guaranteed business results. Do not invent an official product tier.

**Social post:** one idea, a concrete moment and the trainer's own perspective. Avoid
hashtag piles and "excited to announce" filler. Use supplied facts and permissions for
people, client names and photographs. Drafting never authorizes posting.

## Claims and review

1. Keep facts from the supplied materials; never create counts, ratings, testimonials,
   customer names or savings estimates. Mark a user-supplied claim as unchecked if needed.
2. This starter pack contains no live company fact database or policy source. It holds no client price list: the trainer sets and supplies their own price. The fees trainers pay Adaptig are in `pricing-explainer` and never go into client copy.
   Ask for current approved material when the exact official statement matters. Use neutral
   language while drafting, or flag the specific statement that needs verification.
3. Reuse client/participant permission already established for the intended audience. If
   it is missing, anonymize the draft and identify what clearance is needed before sharing.
4. Read the result as the recipient: what happened, why does it matter, what do I do?
   Cut anything that does not help them answer those questions.

## Deliver

Return the finished draft with its subject/headline if useful. Mention only unresolved
facts, placeholders or review needed to use it. Never send or publish unless explicitly
requested by the user. Do not claim company approval or consent that was not supplied.

## Language

Answer the trainer in the language they write to you in. Write anything a client will read in the language the trainer names; when they name none, use the language of their brief or notes.


## Self-Improvement Loop

Suggest a source-repository improvement only when a real run reveals a repeatable issue.
Most runs need no suggestion. Never silently change an installed skill.
