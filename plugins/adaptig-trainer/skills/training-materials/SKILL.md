# Training materials

Turn an approved workshop plan into finished materials a trainer and participants can use: a visual workshop preview, readable participant sheets, complete exercise inputs, facilitator notes and a no-tool alternative. With file tools, produce and show the requested designed artifacts by default. A Markdown draft, list of suggested slides or visual brief is an intermediate source, not the finished visual workshop.

This skill also covers readiness and session planning. It makes teaching materials,
not a sales deck.

This starter contains production instructions and templates, not Adaptig's licensed curriculum. Use the current approved deck and trainer-provided material. Do not claim newly generated content is approved or certified.

## Establish the source and output

Read the supplied plan, approved source deck/exercises and readiness notes. Record source names and revisions. Identify the audience, roles, experience, language, learning outcomes, total time and allowed participant tools/account tier. Ask for missing essentials in one batch.

Infer the required artifacts from the request: worksheet, exercise pack, workshop preview, facilitator notes, one-page handout or a complete pack. Ask only if the choice materially changes the work. Use the trainer's existing presentation format if supplied and supported by the host; otherwise use the bundled visual HTML route. Produce a storyboard only when it is requested or useful as an intermediate production plan. Do not force a slide deck where a worksheet is enough.

**Quick version first, when asked for.** When the request or the `prep-workshop` command asks for a quick version, give only this, in the chat, on one screen: the timed plan as a table (minutes, block, what participants do, with the total), each exercise in one line, and the three things to check before the day. Then ask once whether to build the full pack, and stop. It takes a minute instead of ten, and the trainer corrects the plan before the materials are built on it. Everything below applies once the full pack is requested.

Reuse source approval and artifact instructions already established for this version. Once the full pack is requested, creating a local preview from an authorised brief is part of the work; do not stop to ask again whether to render it. Missing commercial approval does not block designing supported learning materials. Tell the trainer in chat which content is proposed or not yet checked against approved curriculum, and continue the useful draft.

If approved content is missing, build only the parts supported by the brief, using synthetic inputs, and tell the trainer in chat that it is draft material not checked against approved curriculum, with a source gap list. Do not invent official module content, required products, prices or certification language. Source files may come from the trainer's authorized access; do not request internal company systems or share-platform credentials.

## What clients and participants see

Every rendered document is client- or participant-facing: its header, footer, labels and
facilitator note included. Review status, source gaps, rehearsal state, unknowns and open
questions go to the trainer in the chat reply, never into the document. Do not write
status labels ("draft material", "ready for your review", "not approved curriculum"),
gap tags ("— unconfirmed", "(proposed)") or bracketed slots into rendered text; write
what is known in the reader's terms and leave the rest out. The renderer rejects that
wording.

The first time "Adaptig", "Spark", "Shift" or "Shape" appears in a client-facing
document, give it one plain clause, using only these meanings
(`brand-voice/references/trainer-context.md`, `warm-outreach/references/selling.md`):

- **Adaptig**: an AI-adoption training company; independent trainers deliver its approved workshops.
- **Spark**: hands-on workshops that give all staff practical AI skills.
- **Shift**: a small group of champions from the client's own team build real use cases and spread them through the business.
- **Shape**: the leadership team sets direction, priorities and the rules for AI.

A role such as "Adaptig Certified Trainer" in the footer counts as a mention: explain
Adaptig in the subtitle, the opening or `sourceNote`, which prints before the sender line.

Use Toolbox content only through supplied current exports or a connected source the
trainer has explicitly authorised. Record source title, revision and module/slide IDs.
The plugin has no automatic Toolbox connection. If the source is
missing, ask for the relevant export and continue the supported draft. Do not claim
that plausible exercises were retrieved from the official Toolbox.

## Prepare a runnable session

Make a compact readiness list from actual inputs: participants and starting ability,
permitted tools/account tiers, access, language/accessibility, delivery format, room or
call setup, timing, current source materials and the actual learning goal. Mark each
item checked, unchecked or unresolved according to evidence; a checklist is not proof
that access, equipment or a rehearsal works.

Build a timed programme before producing the material. For each block include minutes,
source, participant action/output, facilitation cue and a learning check. Include
transitions and breaks; show that the total equals the requested duration. Keep short
explanations, guided practice, independent practice and debrief proportionate to the
source curriculum and learner readiness. Protect the final takeaway/application.

Plan two concrete recovery moves for likely failures, such as blocked tool access or
an exercise taking longer than expected. Rehearse only when the tools/environment
actually permit it, and report what was observed. Use
[the readiness and programme template](references/preparation-programme.md) as the
editable content spine. Do not add a new official curriculum or an unnecessary task list.

## Build the material

1. **Trace each block to an outcome.** Show the source module/slide or trainer-approved plan item, the participant action, the artifact produced and the check for success. Keep the approved sequence and content; adapt contextual examples within the guidance. Flag a requested change to the curriculum for review.
2. **Use a short learning sequence.** Where it fits the source, use Adaptig's existing Knowledge → Relevance → Playbook → Exercise → Takeaway spine: a concise explanation, why it matters to this work, a repeatable method, participant practice and a usable takeaway. Do not present this five-step spine as the entire current official curriculum.
3. **Write complete exercise instructions.** Include a synthetic input, task, sample prompt/steps, expected output characteristics, a quality rubric, minutes, common failure and debrief. The example output is illustrative, not a claim about what the current tool will return. Mark tool-specific steps for testing on the participant's actual tier.
4. **Differentiate without changing the goal.** Beginners get a prompt starter, glossary where needed and a worked example. Experienced participants get fewer hints and a challenge: compare methods, diagnose a misleading output or apply a tighter constraint. Mixed groups share the same core task and success check; use optional extensions instead of a second unapproved workshop.
5. **Produce participant and facilitator versions.** Keep answers/hints and coaching prompts in the facilitator notes. The participant worksheet should be short enough to use during the exercise; split multi-exercise packs into clear sheets rather than shrinking type. Include an accessible text version. Use [the worksheet template](references/participant-worksheet.md) as a content source, then lay out the participant material as a readable artifact. Clearly separate the facilitator notes in the rendered pack.
6. **Make slides earn their place.** Use [the slide storyboard](references/slides-storyboard.md) internally to specify purpose, visible content, speaker/demo notes, activity, timing and source. Prefer one idea per slide and short demonstrations followed by practice. For an existing approved deck, edit the actual deck when requested and supported; otherwise return the precise edit plan plus a rendered HTML workshop preview. Never claim the source deck was edited when it was not.
7. **Make the no-tool version runnable.** Supply the input plus two saved/synthetic example outputs to evaluate against the same rubric, or a paper workflow that preserves the judgment being taught. State which hands-on outcome remains untested without tool access; do not describe a paper exercise as proof of independent tool use.
8. **Finish with transfer and feedback.** One next-workday use of the method, what to inspect in the output, and a practical learning check. Use only a supplied verified feedback destination; never invent a platform link or make submitting feedback a prerequisite for the artifact.

For a starter exercise that can be adapted, read [the synthetic staff-message example](references/synthetic-exercise.md). Keep its inputs marked as invented. It is a production example, not approved Adaptig curriculum: say so to the trainer in chat, not in the material.

## Render the visual artifact

Use host-native document/slide creation when it supports the requested editable format. Otherwise read [the artifact schema](references/artifact-schema.md), populate a task-specific JSON file and run the bundled [pack renderer](tools/render_pack.py). Set `lang` to the client's language tag, and take `preparedBy`, `role` and `contact` from the trainer's saved details ([trainer-details.md](../brand-voice/references/trainer-details.md)) without asking again. It creates self-contained HTML with bundled visual tokens; it does not require another repository or an external brand service. Follow the schema's actual command and required fields.

The renderer creates `index.html` plus the supported document files for supplied content: `workshop.html`, `proposal.html`, `one-pager.html` and `linkedin.html`. Follow the schema's document-selection rules and supply only the requested content; do not invent unrelated material to create extra tabs. It rejects unsupported JSON fields. Extend the generated HTML or use a suitable native layout when the requested content exceeds the schema; adding an unsupported field cannot make that content appear.

A complete workshop pack includes the visual workshop and participant material; add the proposal, client one-pager or LinkedIn preview when requested. Hand over the requested pieces first. Link the actual output files and show the preview through the host's visual surface when available. A sandbox file link alone must not be described as an inline preview if the host did not display it.

Design the workshop for use, not decoration: a clear opening outcome; a timed agenda; distinct practice cards with the actual input/task/output and success criteria; foundation/stretch paths; visible debrief prompts; printable participant space; and facilitator answers in their own section. Use hierarchy, generous readable type, contrast and print styles rather than squeezing the content into a mock slide. A one-page handout must be concise enough to fit its intended page; it is not the entire workbook reduced in size.

Preserve the editable JSON and HTML alongside the preview. The generated HTML is the finished HTML deliverable; call it a PDF, PowerPoint or native Slides deck only after that format has actually been exported and checked. If PDF output is requested and a supported exporter is available, export the HTML or native document and inspect the result.

## Inspect and hand over

Read the participant instructions as if you had no prior explanation. Check that the input is present, timing fits the plan, the rubric matches the outcome, answers are separate, no private data remains and links/assets actually resolve. For tool-dependent instructions, report whether the trainer has tested them; never infer success from a plausible prompt.

When preview/browser/image tools are available, open every generated artifact, check screen and print layouts, and fix clipped text, overlaps, tiny type, empty sections, broken assets and accidental placeholders. Check narrow screens as well as the presentation size. Compare visible content against the authorised brief; formatting cannot repair an unsupported claim. If visual inspection is unavailable, still deliver the files and state that their layout has not been visually checked. Renderer success alone is not visual validation.

Save the requested editable files in the user's chosen location, preserve earlier versions and give actual paths. Hand over the designed artifact first, then the editable source and, in chat only, a short status: actual formats, inspected views, source/version, what is not yet checked and only material decisions left. Do not offer to create visuals later when the user already requested them.

Use bundled `brand-voice` for copy and `branding-guidelines` for colour, logo, icon and
type rules. The renderer supplies a portable handout layout; use `explainer-one-pager`
when its richer publisher is requested. No external publishing, platform uploads,
shared-file edits or messages occur without a specific instruction.

## Failure and fallback

No approved curriculum: produce the material from the brief and list the source gaps for the trainer in chat. No native preview: create the HTML files and supply working links; identify the missing inline-preview capability. No Python execution but file writing works: create the self-contained HTML directly from the supported content and preserve the source. No file or rendering tools at all: provide complete editable text and explain that a designed artifact could not be produced in this host. Do not call this fallback a finished visual pack, PDF or slide deck.

## Never

- Replace an approved curriculum, certify generated material, or invent a claim, testimonial or learner result.
- Include real client/student/employee data in reusable examples, or assume a trainer account grants participants the same feature.
- Claim a deck, PDF, upload or rehearsal exists when only a plan or source text was produced.
- Sacrifice participant practice to fit more presentation content; cut optional explanation first.

## Language

Answer the trainer in the language they write to you in. Write anything a client will read in the language the trainer names; when they name none, use the language of their brief or notes. [languages.md](../brand-voice/references/languages.md) says how to choose it, how to write Traditional Chinese (Hong Kong), and how to set the language of a file.


## Self-Improvement Loop

Suggest one material change only when a real run reveals a repeatable defect: an unclear instruction, an inaccessible worksheet or an example that no longer works. Show the proposed revision with synthetic content and why it improves the next run. Do not change an installed copy or file an issue silently.
