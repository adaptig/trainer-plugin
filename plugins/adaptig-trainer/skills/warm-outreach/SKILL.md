# Warm outreach

Write the short message that gets a trainer a conversation, or keeps one going. Most
first clients come from people who already trust the trainer, so this skill starts
there. It needs no tools: the message arrives as text in the chat, ready to copy.

Read [selling.md](references/selling.md) first. It is the shared page on how Adaptig
trainers sell, and the proposal workflow reads it too.

## What it writes

| The trainer says | Write |
|---|---|
| "Tell my clients what I do now", "message my network" | **Share, don't pitch.** A note to someone who already knows them: what they are now doing, one concrete thing it does for a team like the reader's, and an open door. No hard ask. |
| "Ask for an introduction" | **Introduction request.** Who they would like to meet and why, plus two forwardable sentences the contact can pass on unchanged. |
| "Follow up after my call" | **Next-day follow-up.** What the client said matters to them, in their words; what was agreed; one useful thing (an answer, an example, a one-page summary); the next step with a date. |
| "They went quiet", "chase the proposal" | **Useful nudge.** Something tied to the last conversation, and an easy way to say it is no longer a priority. Never "just checking in". |
| "Ask for a referral" | **Referral ask.** Sent right after a good workshop: thanks, one real moment from the session, and who else in their world has the same problem. |
| "Review my message" | **Review.** One verdict line, at most three notes that each quote the line they are about, then a tighter version. |

## How to work

1. Use what the trainer already gave. Ask one question only when the message cannot be
   written without it: who is it for, and how do you know them? For a detail only the
   trainer has (a moment from the session, what the client said mattered), write the
   message with one marked gap for it and say so; do not hold the message back.
2. Write the message. Keep it short enough to read on a phone: about 90 words for an
   email or a LinkedIn message, under 200 characters for a LinkedIn connection note.
3. Open with a reason that is about the reader. Make one small ask. Name a date or a
   time frame. Write in the trainer's own voice and language; `brand-voice` covers tone.
4. Under the message, add two lines and nothing more: what to do if there is no reply,
   and when.

When the trainer asks for several messages (for example to ten contacts), write each one
from its own real connection. A message that could go to all ten unchanged is a
broadcast; say so and personalise it.

## Rules

- **Only real connections and real facts.** Do not invent a shared history, a mutual
  contact, a result or a client name. If the trainer gave no link to the person, say so
  and ask who in their network knows them. With no link at all, give both
  in the same reply: the question about who could introduce them, and one short, honest
  first message, with the note that cold messages are rarely answered.
- **No number, claim or client name** unless the trainer supplied it or it comes from
  current approved Adaptig material. A named client needs that client's written yes.
- **The journey, lightly.** When the conversation is already about training, mention that
  a first workshop is usually the start of a longer path. Do not unload the whole
  catalogue into a first message.
- **No price in a first message to a company.** The trainer's fee belongs in the
  conversation and the proposal. An open workshop with a ticket price is different: the
  price is part of the announcement.
- **Rhythm for follow-ups:** within a day or two of a call; then about weekly for three
  or four weeks, each time with something useful; then leave it for a couple of months.
  If they declined, stop.
- **Drafts only.** The trainer sends. Never send, schedule, connect or post.

## What happens next

Offer one next step, once, in plain words, only when the run calls for it and only when
that skill is installed or attached in this conversation; otherwise name it as another
skill in this plugin:

- A first call went well, whether or not they asked for an offer: "Want the proposal
  drafted now, so it goes out today?"
- The client wants to show colleagues: "Want a one-page summary they can forward?"
- A meeting is booked: "Want three to five slides for it?"

Otherwise stop.

## Failure and fallback

No information about the person: give a fill-in version with the gaps marked, labelled
as not ready to send. No browsing: use only what the trainer supplied. A request to send
or to message strangers in bulk: decline that part, and offer the warm version.

## Language

Answer the trainer in the language they write to you in. Write anything a client will read in the language the trainer names; when they name none, use the language of their brief or notes. [languages.md](../brand-voice/references/languages.md) says how to choose it, how to write Traditional Chinese (Hong Kong), and how to set the language of a file.


## Self-Improvement Loop

Suggest one change only when a real run shows a repeatable gap that would change a future run: a message type trainers keep asking for, or a rule that produced a worse message. Describe it with invented names; never include a real contact, client or message in a suggestion. Do not silently alter an installed skill.
