# How Adaptig trainers sell

The short version of what Adaptig teaches its trainers about winning clients, from the
trainer business-development sessions and the Trainer Toolbox. Every sales workflow in
this pack reads this page: outreach and the proposal. It holds no
prices and no client names.

## Sell the journey, not one workshop

A single workshop is a lot of work for a few hours. The lasting result, and the larger
engagement, is the whole adoption journey.

| Stage | Who it is for | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Spark** | Everyone | Hands-on workshops that give all staff practical AI skills |
| **Shift** | A small group of internal champions | They build real use cases and spread them through the business |
| **Shape** | The leadership team | Leaders set direction, priorities and the rules for AI |
| **Show** | A wider audience | A keynote that opens the door to the stages above |

- Present Spark as the first step, with Shift and Shape as the next phases.
- Make the first yes small: one team, one workshop, a short pilot. Then widen.
- Describe a stage by who it is for and what changes for them. Take exact durations,
  group sizes and inclusions from the current one-pagers in the Trainer Toolbox, never
  from memory. Older material describes Shift and Shape differently.
- Not certified to deliver a stage yet? You can still sell it. Deliver it together with
  a certified trainer or bring Adaptig in, and say that plainly to the client. Do not
  make the client wait, and do not quote someone else's fee: call that stage "priced
  separately".

## Ten rules

1. Warm introductions beat cold outreach. Start with people who already trust you, and
   ask them to introduce you one level up.
2. Chase organisations that are already curious about AI. Go in through the person who
   owns the result: the chief executive, the finance lead or a business-unit head.
3. On the first call, do not lead with AI. Ask questions until they ask what you do.
   If you talk more than 40% of the time, you are talking too much.
4. Sell the whole journey, and get the leaders' commitment from the start.
5. Sell the outcome and the feeling of being able to do the work, not the tools.
6. Never argue with an objection. Acknowledge, reframe, redirect.
7. Send the proposal the same day or the next, whether or not they asked for one.
8. Follow up within a day or two, with something useful. Never "just checking in".
9. End every conversation with one next step and a date.
10. Ask for a referral right after a good workshop, and ask more than once.

## The first call

Find out, in their words:

- what prompted their interest, and what success would look like
- who would take part: departments, roles, how many people
- how much they use AI today, and which tools their IT allows
- the date, the place and how soon they want to move
- who decides

Close it one of three ways, each with a date:

- **Interested:** promise the proposal within 48 hours, and send it sooner.
- **Others must agree:** offer a one-page summary or a short call with the decision maker.
- **Not ready:** send something useful and agree when you will talk again.

## Objections

Say "I understand" or "that's a fair point" first. Then reframe.

| They say | Principle | Where to take it |
|---|---|---|
| "It's too expensive." | Move from cost to return | Ask what they compare it with, then work through the time their own team would get back |
| "No budget right now." | Not now is not no | Ask when the next budget cycle starts and offer a one-page summary to build the case |
| "Can you do it cheaper?" | Change the scope, not your rate | Adjust group size, format or scope to fit their range |
| "AI isn't relevant to us." | Offer proof, don't argue | Show a use case from their own industry |
| "We're not ready yet." | Question the premise gently | Ask what ready would look like |
| "We already did AI training." | Be the next level | Ask how much people use AI day to day since then |
| "People can learn this online." | Behaviour change is the difference | Live practice on their own work, with someone in the room |
| "We can't spare half a day." | Time spent is time earned back | Compare the session with the hours it saves each week after |
| "What makes you different?" | Know your three points | Live and hands-on, works with their approved tools, a method that goes from first use to strategy |

Use a number only when it comes from current approved Adaptig material or from the
client's own figures. Do not quote savings or completion statistics from memory.

## After the workshop

- Ask for a referral while the room is still warm. The person who hired you is most
  willing straight afterwards.
- About 30 days later, check in on how people use what they learned. That conversation
  opens the next phase.
- Match the next proposal to what you saw: more teams when satisfaction was high, Shift
  when a few people are clearly ahead, Shape when the leaders start asking about rules
  and direction.

## Your price

You are independent and you set your own price. Price the outcome, not the hours. When
a client pushes back, change the scope before you change your rate.
