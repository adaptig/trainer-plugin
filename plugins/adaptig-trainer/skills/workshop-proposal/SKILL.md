# Workshop proposal

Turn one client's discovery brief into a concrete, designed training proposal the trainer can read, adjust and send. Use the existing Adaptig content spine and evidence checks for trainer-owned work, with a finished visual layout by default when file tools are available. This workflow grants no access to company pricing and no authority to commit Adaptig; its generated layout is a proposal design, not an official contract template.

## Text first, and one question about the fee

Give the trainer a usable proposal in the conversation first: the full client-facing text, ready to paste into an email or a document. Render the designed file after that, when file tools exist. A trainer in a chat app must never wait on a renderer to read their own proposal.

The trainer is independent and sets their own price. When the brief holds no fee, do not hold the proposal back: give the full proposal text without the investment section, and end that same reply with the one question, "What is your fee for this, and in which currency?" Their answer is the price; add the investment section and send the finished text. Do not ask who approved it. In that same question, and only when the brief lacks them, ask for the other things the client copy needs: whether tax is added on top of the fee, and what goes in the signature (the name that signs, their role, the contact details to show, and the date to put on the proposal). Ask once; never print a placeholder. The client copy never carries a bracketed gap; a missing detail is left out and listed for the trainer in chat.

## Input and output

Start from the trainer's supplied or explicitly authorised call notes, client email, discovery brief or earlier proposal. Use only this client's material. Nothing else is needed: no Adaptig account, CRM or price book.

Produce three clearly separated parts:

1. **Client-facing artifact:** a polished, editable HTML proposal with preview by default, or the requested native document/deck when supported. A proposal-email request instead gets finished email copy; do not force an attachment. All formats share one content spine. Markdown and a slide outline are production sources, not final designed proposals.
2. **Trainer review, in chat only:** sources, assumptions, missing decisions, price lines still unknown and price calculations. Most open points are the trainer's own to decide; name another person only when the brief does.
3. **Handover, in chat only:** exact status, actual format/file, changes since the previous version and one useful next action. Never send, publish, change sharing or create an email draft in another app.

Parts 2 and 3 never go into the client document, its header or footer, or any rendered field. The client reads a finished proposal from the trainer: no review status, "draft" label, "for discussion" box, "unconfirmed" cell or internal note.

Read [intake-and-pricing.md](references/intake-and-pricing.md) for the brief and commercial rules, then [proposal-spine.md](references/proposal-spine.md) for the complete structure and editable template. Synthetic acceptance checks are in [acceptance-scenarios.md](references/acceptance-scenarios.md).

## 1. Establish a useful brief

Read the material already available before asking. Identify the client and audience, what they need to do differently, current skill level, delivery constraints, desired scope and who reviews the offer. Ask at most three focused questions for material gaps. Experienced participants need a stretch path; do not default everyone to an introductory workshop.

Show a short brief with each field marked **confirmed**, **proposed** or **unknown**, with its source. A prior budget or old quote is context, not a current approved fee. A client's wish is not an Adaptig commitment. Do not invent names, roles, dates, participant counts, credentials, product facts or client proof.

Proceed with a useful scope even if commercial inputs are missing. Write proposed elements as what the trainer proposes ("I propose two sessions"), not with review tags. Put unknowns in the trainer review in chat; omit unsupported specifics from the client text. If the intended audience and problem are unknown, provide a brief/template and targeted questions instead of fabricating a personalised programme.

## 2. Design the offer from the learning need

For each component, specify:

- the audience and one observable outcome;
- the proposed format, duration and capacity, supported by the brief or explicitly labelled for trainer confirmation;
- what participants practise and make, including a task appropriate to their experience;
- what the trainer prepares, what the client supplies, what is included and what is excluded;
- how learning is checked and what happens after the session.

Use `training-materials` when a timed plan or readiness check is needed. Keep proposal-level
detail here: a short sequence and representative exercise are enough. Do not claim a
runbook, deck, workbook, certificate or functioning tool exists merely because it is scoped.

Integrate exercises and materials as deliverables, not vague extras. State the proposed
number/type, format, audience, customisation source, timing, revision boundary and owner
when known. `training-materials` covers preparation and teaching artifacts. Use synthetic/anonymised practice
data by default; client data requires an authorised use and tool environment.

For official Adaptig programme names, inclusions, capacity or credentials, use current trainer-approved product material. Without it, draft a custom workshop in plain language and flag product alignment to the trainer in chat. Never borrow product facts from memory.

Where useful, explain the learning flow as **Knowledge → Relevance → Playbook → Exercise → Takeaway**: introduce the idea, connect it to the audience's work, model a usable method, let participants practise, and leave them with a concrete aid or next action. This is a design aid, not a fixed duration or package promise; adapt its depth to prior experience.

## 3. Build one content spine

Follow the S0–S12 structure in [proposal-spine.md](references/proposal-spine.md): heading, context, programme, value, inclusions, delivery, investment/terms, optional presenter/proof, possible next steps, close and signature. S0 is private drafting context and never copied into the client document.

Already discussed: restate the plan in the client's terms and avoid a generic company introduction. First contact: explain the reason for the proposal and use only approved, sourced company claims if helpful.

The client may never have heard of Adaptig, and its logo is on the page. The first time "Adaptig", "Spark", "Shift" or "Shape" appears in the client document, give it one plain clause, using only these meanings from [trainer-context.md](../brand-voice/references/trainer-context.md) and [selling.md](../warm-outreach/references/selling.md):

| Term | Plain clause for the client |
|---|---|
| Adaptig | an AI-adoption training company; independent trainers deliver its approved workshops |
| Spark | hands-on workshops that give all staff practical AI skills |
| Shift | a small group of champions from your own team build real use cases and spread them through the business |
| Shape | your leadership team sets direction, priorities and the rules for AI |

Once explained, the term can stand alone. Do not make claims about how other organisations buy (no "most organisations start with Spark"), or any other company claim these pages do not support. A revision opens with what changed, without rewriting the earlier agreement as though it had always said that.

Give every component three value angles: what participants can do; what the buyer gains operationally; and how the buyer can explain the programme to colleagues. Outcomes are intended learning results, not guaranteed productivity, sales or learning gains.

Use `brand-voice` for the proposal and cover email, and `branding-guidelines` for its
visual rules. Match the trainer's actual identity, language and authorised facts.
Preserve a supplied approved visual template when supported. Otherwise use the proposal
layout in `training-materials`; absence of a template is not a reason to stop at Markdown.
Do not label that design official letterhead, an approved contract or a signable document.
The one-pager publisher is not a multi-page proposal renderer.

Use relevant Toolbox content from supplied current exports or a specifically authorised
connected source. Keep its source/revision and the client's requirements traceable.
The plugin does not automatically access Toolbox material.

## Present the journey

Read [how Adaptig trainers sell](../warm-outreach/references/selling.md) for the stages: **Spark** gives everyone hands-on AI skills; **Shift** takes a small group of champions from the client's own team who build real use cases and spread them; **Shape** is for the leadership team, who set direction and rules; **Show** is a keynote that opens the door. That page's advice on how clients usually buy is for the trainer, not a claim to print.

By default the proposal shows what can come after this first step, not only a one-off
workshop. The programme holds only what this offer delivers. Later stages go in the
optional **Possible next steps** list (`proposal.possibleNextSteps`), after the programme
and price, unnumbered, one short line each: who it is for and what changes for them,
with the plain clause above, and that it is not part of this offer. Never put a later
stage in the numbered programme or the price table. Leave the list out when the trainer
asks for that, or when the client has already said no to it.

Price only what the trainer delivers and has given a fee for. A later stage the trainer
would deliver with a certified trainer or with Adaptig gets no figure: write "not part of
this offer; priced separately if you want it". Take durations, group sizes and inclusions
for each stage from the current Toolbox one-pagers; without them, describe the stage in
plain words and flag the detail for the trainer in chat. Never state a remembered offer as
a current one.

## Contracts are not in this version

This version makes proposals only. When a trainer asks for a contract, service agreement,
NDA or an agreement with another trainer, say that contracts are not part of this version
of the skill and that they should ask Adaptig. Do not draft one.

## 3a. Produce the visual proposal

Read the companion [artifact schema](../training-materials/references/artifact-schema.md) and use [the bundled renderer](../training-materials/tools/render_pack.py) to produce `proposal.html` from the supplied proposal content. Use the schema's actual command, document-selection rules and fields. Keep the JSON editable. Do not invent an unrelated workshop, social post or one-pager to fill other sections; render the requested artifact on its own when appropriate.

Keep every required proposal section visible. Use the schema's structured `proposal.commercial` fields for approved pricing/terms; do not flatten an investment table into a subtitle or omit material terms to fit the design. It displays supplied line totals, terms and validity without establishing their arithmetic or authority. Verify calculations before supplying them. A line whose amount is not known is left out of the client document and listed for the trainer in chat; the renderer rejects a row without a total. The renderer rejects unsupported JSON fields: when required details exceed its supported layout, extend the generated HTML or use an appropriate native layout. Verify the final visible document against the full content spine. Native document/slide tools may replace the HTML route, but still produce the actual file and inspect its rendered pages.

Use a clear cover/title, a short client-specific context, distinct outcome/programme blocks, tangible exercise/material deliverables, a readable delivery/responsibility section and one clear next decision. Include a separate investment table only when authorised inputs support it. An unpriced proposal can still be professionally designed: say in one plain client sentence that the price follows once the scope is agreed, with no status label. Keep private drafting context, calculation workings, status and approval questions in the chat reply, outside the client document.

The proposal shows who sends it and when. Fill `preparedBy` (the name that signs), `role`, `contact` and `date` from the trainer's answers, and `proposal.signOff` with a closing line in the client's language. The renderer prints the name and date beside the title, a signature block after the next step and a sender line in the footer, and refuses a proposal without a name and date. Do not repeat the name in `nextStep`. If the trainer has not given these, ask once in chat; never print placeholder text.

Open the actual artifact and show it through the host's preview surface when available. Inspect every page/view for readable type, whitespace, coherent hierarchy, clipped content, table overflow and broken assets; check print layout when a printable document is requested. Fix visible issues before handover. When PDF is requested and export tools exist, create and inspect the PDF too. Do not call HTML a PDF or a slide outline a deck.

This renderer lives in the sibling `training-materials` skill, included in the full plugin. If only this skill's individual ZIP is installed, identify that missing dependency and obtain the approved sibling pack or create a self-contained HTML layout directly when the host supports it. Never silently claim the renderer ran.

## 4. Handle pricing without inventing it

The trainer sets their own price. Prices, currency, units, minima, discounts, taxes, expenses, payment terms and validity come from the trainer: from the brief, or from the one fee question at the top of this skill. Their answer is the price for this proposal; note the date they gave it. Never ask who approved it, and never invent, estimate or round a figure.

Three prices are not the trainer's to quote: Adaptig's own fees, another trainer's fee, and the price of a stage someone else will deliver. Describe those as next phases, priced separately.

When a price or term is missing and the trainer wants to decide later, keep drafting the useful scope. Mark the handover **Unpriced scope draft**. Omit the investment table or state plainly that the document describes scope and does not include a price offer. Do not fill missing values with sample amounts, zeros, invented ranges or accidental commitments.

When inputs are supplied, follow [intake-and-pricing.md](references/intake-and-pricing.md): compute each extension and total with decimal arithmetic or a trusted calculation tool; show the calculation in the trainer review. Keep currencies separate unless the trainer supplies a conversion. A proposed budget is not a fee. Missing calculator support means totals remain unverified and the draft cannot be called commercially checked.

## 5. Check before handover

Check both the draft and any generated file:

| Check | Pass condition |
|---|---|
| Brief and facts | Specifics trace to this client's authorised material, current approved offer info or labelled trainer proposals; conflicts remain visible for review. |
| Learning and scope | Each component has an observable outcome, appropriate practice, an output/check, inclusions and clear client/trainer responsibilities. Proposed exercise/material scope is feasible and not described as already built. |
| Commercials | The trainer's prices, units and currency agree; totals match tool results; taxes, expenses, assumptions, payment and validity are sourced or explicitly absent from an unpriced scope draft. |
| Buyer read | A decision maker can understand what they get, why it matters, what they must provide and what decisions remain. Fix wording; never alter price or scope just to remove a blocker. |
| Privacy and proof | No other client's names, fees, quotes or materials; only permissioned claims. No invented testimonial, participant count or credential. |
| Output | All requested sections exist; sender, date and signature are present; no bracketed slots, review status, "draft"/"for discussion" labels, "unconfirmed" cells, private notes or unsupported attachment claims in the client copy. If rendered, inspect every page/slide for clipping and collisions; otherwise say layout was not reviewed. |
| Plain words | Adaptig, Spark, Shift and Shape each get their plain clause at first mention; no unsourced claim about how other organisations buy. |
| Trainer review | The trainer has read this version. A changed price or scope is shown to them again before it reaches the client. |
| Journey | Later stages appear only under Possible next steps, unnumbered and outside the programme and price table, unless the trainer asked to leave them out. No price for a stage the trainer does not deliver. |

Tell the trainer the exact, honest status in chat: **Scope draft**, **Unpriced scope draft**, **Priced draft — ready for your review**, or **Final — confirmed by the trainer on [date]** only when the trainer has said so for this version. A status never goes into the client document, its JSON's rendered fields, header or footer. An earlier yes does not cover a changed price or scope. Never claim a legal or company sign-off check you did not perform.

## 6. Hand over and revise

Lead with the actual visual proposal and its working file link/preview, followed by the editable source and, in chat, the trainer review: status, open decisions, price lines left out and gaps. List only material open decisions and their proposed owner; if the role is unknown, say so. Report which formats and layouts were actually checked. A short cover-email draft may accompany the proposal; mention an attachment only when it exists or mark the attachment line for completion in the trainer review.

For a revision, work from the supplied version, identify changed scope/materials/commercials, preserve the sent record and show a concise change list. Never silently replace a price the client already saw. An added workshop, exercise pack or follow-up changes delivery work and needs explicit scope/commercial review.

Complete all requested proposal and visual work before suggesting a next step. Reuse source, scope and rendering approval already given for this version; do not ask the trainer to approve creating a local preview again. A changed price or scope goes back to the trainer before the client sees it. This skill sends nothing and updates no tracking system. The trainer sends the proposal themselves, the same day or the next.

## Failure and fallback

No browsing: use supplied evidence and omit unchecked public claims. No authorised client source: make a reusable proposal outline and ask for the brief. No fee yet: ask the one fee question; if the trainer wants to decide later, deliver the unpriced scope draft. No native document tools: use the HTML route. No file/rendering tools at all: the text already given in the conversation is the deliverable; say the designed file could not be produced in this host and do not call the text a rendered proposal. No visual inspection: deliver the actual file and state layout inspection is pending. Conflicting inputs: identify the conflict and continue with unaffected sections; do not choose a commercial value yourself.

## Language

Answer the trainer in the language they write to you in. Write anything a client will read in the language the trainer names; when they name none, use the language of their brief or notes.


## Self-Improvement Loop

Suggest a source-repository improvement only when a real run reveals a repeatable gap that would change future behaviour. Include a redacted example and the affected step; never include real client fees or private materials in an issue. Do not silently alter an installed skill. A price is the trainer's own and never goes into this skill as a reusable figure.
